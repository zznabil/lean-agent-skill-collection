## Conditional execution controls
- For normative requirements, use BCP 14 (RFC 2119/8174) uppercase MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT and MAY. Preserve requirement force. State one actor, action and observable verification target.
- For critical or risky work only, place warnings before hazards and hold points before critical or irreversible steps. Before destructive or hazardous work, verify actual state.
- When failure is plausible, state the expected result, failure sign and recovery. For difficult mechanisms, explain simple foundations first. When useful, contrast noncompliant and compliant code or configuration.
- For critical procedures, use Summary, Prerequisites, WARNING, Steps, PAUSE/VERIFY, Expected Result and Recovery where useful. Add contrast or TL;DR only when helpful.
- For measurable multi-step agent work, use a truthful named 20-cell ASCII progress format when applicable.

## Task and boundary
- When a project enters a safety-critical or regulated domain, determine its required authoritative workflow.
- Do not treat this generic register category as a single named standard. Do not invent domain certification requirements.
- Limit work to the selected artifact or assessment. This routine does not permit attacks, deployment, publication or policy changes.
- OFF-DEFAULT GUARD: apply this routine only to the stated selection or review request. Do not install it as an always-on workflow.

## Source and limits
- Read [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md). Identify the source, applicable edition or part, available originals, access limits and copying terms.

## Procedure
1. Identify the product, intended use, hazards, deployment setting, jurisdiction and responsible domain authority.
2. Obtain applicable laws, standards, editions and contractual requirements from authorised sources and qualified reviewers.
3. Keep source discovery separate from the determination of legal or regulatory applicability.
4. Record the established safety lifecycle, assurance evidence, independence requirements and approval responsibilities.
5. Maintain traceability from hazards and risk controls to implementation and verification.
6. Do not claim that generic Lean, a language pass, unit tests or a standards checklist satisfies the domain process.
7. Conduct safe preparatory analysis. Do not conduct unauthorised consequential actions.
8. Escalate unresolved safety-critical requirements to the designated qualified owner.
9. Record limitations and hold points that prevent release or use. Do not waive them to finish the task.
10. Return the domain-source and responsibility map. This heterogeneous register category has no canonical PDF.

## Verify and recover
- **Worked check (illustrative, not executed):** A medical-device feature is declared safe because it passed the general code-review checklist.
- **Expected:** Require evidence and the actual qualified domain assessment. The generic checklist cannot replace them.
- **If blocked:** If the applicable jurisdiction, product classification or qualified approval authority is unknown, stop the affected assessment or action. Record the missing prerequisite. Request it from the responsible owner. Continue only independent, authorised work.
- Preserve facts, identifiers, links, required checks, permissions, negation, exceptions and failure/recovery paths.
- Distinguish planned work, actual evidence and unknown results. Missing or stale evidence does not establish a pass.
- Do not remove requirements to improve a score or meet the line budget. Record any unresolved source or task conflict.
- Use the smallest check that can detect the relevant defect. A schema, linter or inventory alone cannot prove task success.

- Return the scoped result, evidence, unresolved requirements and next permitted action.
- Make one correction pass. Recheck the affected evidence. If a required issue remains, report it. Do not loop or claim completion.
- Identify the checked scope, source edition, evidence and limits. Do not claim formal conformance or model-behaviour improvement from this routine.
