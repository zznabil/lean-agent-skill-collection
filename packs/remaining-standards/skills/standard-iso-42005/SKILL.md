# ISO/IEC 42005 AI impact assessment

## Task and boundary
- Prepare a scoped AI-system impact assessment with the responsible project stakeholders.
- Do not treat a public abstract or this routine as the full ISO/IEC 42005 standard.
- Work only on the selected artifact or assessment. This routine does not grant permission to run attacks, deploy, publish or change
  policy.

## Source and limits
- Read [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md) for the selected edition, official files, source boundaries and copying terms.
- Read the relevant source sections before making a source-specific decision. A compact routine is not the complete specification.
- Historical adoption decision: Conditional benchmark. Keep this boundary unless an authorised decision explicitly changes it.
- Full licensed text was not obtained. This is a Lean application routine grounded in public scope and existing Lean guidance, not a
  clause transcript.
- Obtain the authorised full text before a clause-level assessment. Do not invent definitions, thresholds or conformity results.

## Procedure
1. Confirm the AI system, intended use, lifecycle stage, jurisdictional context and assessment owner.
2. Obtain the applicable authoritative edition before assigning clauses or claiming a complete ISO assessment.
3. Identify people and groups affected directly or indirectly by the system, including non-users.
4. Describe foreseeable beneficial and adverse impacts using evidence and clearly marked assumptions.
5. Consider severity, likelihood, distribution and context of impacts with criteria set by the responsible organisation.
6. Record data/model dependencies, deployment constraints and uncertainty that can change the assessment.
7. Consult relevant stakeholders when required, and do not invent completed consultation.
8. Identify mitigations, accountable owners, residual impacts and escalation or approval decisions.
9. Set review triggers for material changes or newly observed impacts.
10. Report the scope, evidence and unresolved questions; verify the source link independently when the historical catalogue URL
    cannot be retrieved.

## Preserve and verify
- Preserve facts, identifiers, links, required checks, permissions, negation, exceptions and failure/recovery paths.
- Separate planned work, actual evidence and unknown results. Missing or stale evidence is not a pass.
- Do not delete requirements to improve a score or satisfy the line budget; record an unresolved source or task conflict.
- Use the smallest check that can detect the relevant defect. A schema, linter or inventory alone does not prove task success.

## Worked case — authored, not an executed model result
- **Situation:** An impact assessment covers direct users but ignores people whose records influence automated decisions.
- **Expected:** Include the affected non-users and explain the evidence and mitigation gaps.
- **Missing-evidence case:** The full standard or evidence for a claimed impact assessment criterion is unavailable.

## Finish and stop
- Return the scoped result, evidence, unresolved requirements and next permitted action.
- Make one correction pass and recheck affected evidence. If a required issue remains, report it rather than looping or claiming
  completion.
- Identify the checked scope, source edition, evidence and limits. Do not claim formal conformance or model-behaviour improvement
  from this routine.
