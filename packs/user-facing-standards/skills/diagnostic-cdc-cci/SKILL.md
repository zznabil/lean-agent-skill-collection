# CDC Clear Communication Index: scoped diagnosis

## Use
- Assess defined public information with the CDC Clear Communication Index. Deliver item evidence; score only after checking the applicable form and material.
- First identify the audience, health-literacy context, objective and main message. Do not use this diagnostic as a universal release gate.

## Source and scope
- Read the official user guide and the appropriate full or modified score sheet linked in SOURCES.md.
- Web review could access the PDFs. The binary downloader received HTTP 403. This folder contains no PDF.
- If you cannot inspect the form or actual material, provide a qualitative review. State why the score is incomplete.

## Procedure
1. Read all material in its delivered form. Include graphics, headings, numbers and visible links.
2. Record the four introductory answers. Select the score sheet for the material’s format.
3. Assess the main message, call to action, their placement and the visual emphasis that supports them.
4. Assess familiar language, active wording for the main message and action, information chunks and meaningful headings.
5. Assess whether the material represents the state of the science. Include remaining uncertainty.
6. Apply the relevant behavioural-recommendation, number and risk items. Use their actual wording and skip logic.
7. Record the observed passage or design evidence for each applicable item. Assign the required zero-or-one rating.
8. Calculate the percentage using the applicable-item denominator. Show ratings and unresolved evidence so others can check it.
9. Revise evidenced defects and rescore changed material. Do not remove necessary facts to raise the score.

## Keep the task contract
- The official tool has 20 scored items in seven areas. Use the score sheet to determine applicability, not convenience.
- CDC describes 90 or higher as passing within this tool. Do not treat this as a universal Lean acceptance threshold.
- A score does not replace factual checking, formative research or pretesting with the intended audience.
- Do not infer clinical accuracy, behaviour change or WCAG conformance from a communication score.

## Verify and deliver
- Report the form/version, audience, ratings, calculation, diagnostic issues and actual review limits.
- Distinguish a passing index score from unmet task, safety or evidence requirements.
- Deliver useful findings or revised material first. Include the score only if you calculated it.

## Worked distinction
A clear headline scores well, but the body omits what the reader should do next.
Evaluate the call-to-action items and the real task; the headline alone is not acceptance evidence.

## Communication kernel
Trusted root `AGENTS.md` governs when loaded; otherwise use this standalone fallback. Do not claim activation without evidence.
Apply this skill’s source-specific procedure only to its task.
- Use ASD-STE100-inspired short, active sentences and concrete words; retain domain terms.
- Use ISO 704-inspired stable concepts and terminology. Define unfamiliar terms when needed.
- Separate tutorial, how-to, reference and explanation with Diátaxis when helpful, not forced headings.
- Lead with the answer or action, not a required Summary. Use TL;DR only first when helpful for complex material, without a repeated conclusion.
- Keep simple answers short, not arbitrarily truncated. Match requested voice and format. Use British spelling except for sources, syntax or requested locale.
- Preserve facts, quantities, negation, actors, conditions, exceptions, rights, permissions, uncertainty and evidence. Never claim unchecked compliance or completion.
- Preserve normative BCP 14 MUST/SHOULD/MAY force and exceptions. Important requirements name one actor, action and observable check.
- Before hazards, warn with verified risks. At critical steps, hold and verify the safe state. Retain task-specific controls.
- When failure is plausible, give expected results, failure signs and recovery.
- For measurable multi-step work, show a named 20-cell ASCII bar (# processed, - remaining), with floor percentage from durable counts.
- Keep PASS/FAIL/BLOCKED separate. Classify failed, blocked, skipped or untested items with evidence before counting.
- Without a defensible total, report phase, evidence and next action. This does not invoke manual wait-what.
- Explain difficult mechanisms from foundations. Use compliant/noncompliant code or configuration contrasts only when useful.
