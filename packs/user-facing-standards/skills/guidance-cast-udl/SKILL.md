# CAST UDL 3.0: remove learning barriers

## Use
- Design a learning task, resource or assessment for varied learners. Deliver options that connect to a learning goal and observed barriers.
- First state the goal and barriers. Do not apply every option to every learner or task.

## Source and scope
- Read the official 2024 UDL 3.0 graphic organiser linked in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).
- The organiser is an official one-page overview. It does not contain the complete online explanations for all considerations.
- Read the official detailed consideration when its interpretation matters. State if you checked only the overview.

## Procedure
1. Separate the intended learning goal from unnecessary constraints on how learners receive information or respond.
2. For engagement, consider interests and identities, meaningful goals, sustained effort, belonging and emotional capacity.
3. Offer relevant choice, manageable challenge and actionable feedback. Do not substitute entertainment for the goal.
4. For representation, inspect access to perception, language and symbols. Inspect the connections learners need to build knowledge.
5. Clarify unfamiliar vocabulary and notation. Connect prior knowledge and concrete examples to the new concept.
6. For action and expression, inspect access to interaction, communication tools and strategy development.
7. Allow alternative ways to demonstrate the same learning goal if a fixed response method creates an avoidable barrier.
8. Support planning, resource organisation and progress monitoring where learners need those supports.
9. Identify bias or exclusion in the material, expectations and available participation routes.
10. Observe participation and learning for the chosen options. Revise barriers that persist. Choice alone does not show that learners met the goal.

## Keep the task contract
- Do not infer ability, motivation or support needs from a diagnosis alone.
- Do not lower a required learning objective just to make an assessment easier.
- Do not force multiple representations, a quiz or extra choices if they add no useful access.
- An application to agent-facing instructions is an adaptation. It is not evidence that agents learn like people.

## Verify and deliver
- Map each chosen option to a barrier, a UDL consideration and observable evidence.
- Check access and learning separately. Liking a resource does not establish mastery.
- Deliver the learning design and material unresolved barriers. Do not claim universal accessibility.

## Worked distinction
An assessment of causal reasoning unnecessarily requires rapid handwriting.
Consider another response method while preserving the causal-reasoning objective and its evidence requirements.

## Communication kernel
If trusted root `AGENTS.md` loads, follow it; otherwise apply this standalone fallback. Do not claim root activation without evidence.
- **Brevity:** Use the fewest words that complete the task. Start with the answer, result or blocker. Remove filler, repeated conclusions, routine narration and decorative headings. Do not add a **Summary** heading or closing **TL;DR**. Use a leading **TL;DR** only when it improves a complex answer.
- **Language:** Use ASD-STE100-inspired short, active sentences and concrete verbs.
- **Terms:** Use ISO 704-inspired consistent terms: one preferred term per distinct concept. Define unfamiliar terms when needed; preserve exact technical and UI strings.
- **Structure:** Use Diátaxis to separate how-to, reference, explanation and tutorial content only when this helps navigation.
- **Integrity:** Preserve facts, actors, conditions, negation, exceptions, permissions, requirements, source rights, uncertainty, evidence, accessibility and the requested voice or format. Do not omit required details just to shorten text.
- **Safety and proof:** Use BCP 14 force only for normative rules. Keep warning-before-hazard, verified safe state, hold points, expected results, failure signs and recovery when relevant. Report actual test outcomes; do not invent a pass.
These are writing guides, not proof of standards conformance or host behaviour. Task-specific source rules still apply.
## Conditional execution rules
- For unchecked results, do not claim compliance or completion.
- In normative text, preserve BCP 14 MUST/SHOULD/MAY force and exceptions. For important requirements, identify one actor, action and observable check.
- Before a hazardous action, state the verified risk and give a warning. For critical steps, use a hold point and check the safe state. When failure is plausible, state the expected result, failure sign and recovery. These controls do not replace task-specific controls.
- Give progress only when requested or useful. Keep progress distinct from the verdict.
- For a difficult mechanism, explain from foundations. For code or configuration, contrast compliant and noncompliant cases only when useful.
