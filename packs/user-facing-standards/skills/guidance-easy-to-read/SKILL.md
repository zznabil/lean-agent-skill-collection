# Inclusion Europe: Easy-to-Read authoring
## Use
- Use this skill when the request specifies Easy-to-Read or when the intended readers and task justify it. Deliver reviewed material or a draft that explicitly awaits intended-user review.
- Treat Easy-to-Read as a specialised accessible representation. It is not just short text or general plain language.
## Source and scope
- Use Inclusion Europe’s official Information for all booklet. Use the current rules and logo conditions linked in SOURCES.md.
- This pack does not bundle the original PDF. Permission to redistribute it was not established.
- The method requires intended-user involvement. An agent-only wording pass cannot replace that involvement.
## Procedure
1. Identify the intended readers. Identify what they need to know and what they must be able to do.
2. Involve people with relevant intellectual disabilities in planning, drafting or checking, as the method requires.
3. Choose familiar, concrete words. Explain necessary difficult terms. Use the same term for the same concept.
4. State one idea at a time. Use short, clear sentences in a logical order. Identify the actor and action.
5. Use direct, literal statements. Prefer simpler constructions. Do not change prohibitions, exceptions or uncertainty.
6. Use relevant pictures or symbols. They must support the actual meaning and be understandable to the intended readers.
7. Use clear type, consistent alignment and spacing to make the layout easy to follow. Apply the booklet’s rules for the medium.
8. Explain necessary numbers, dates and abbreviations. Retain precise values when they determine safe or correct action.
9. Ask intended readers to explain and use the material. Revise misunderstood content. Repeat the affected task check.
10. Check current attribution, proofreading and logo conditions before claiming Easy-to-Read status or adding the logo.
## Keep the task contract
- Do not infantilise adult readers or assume that they have identical needs. Do not replace the full version without preserving necessary access.
- Do not convert print font sizes directly into arbitrary screen-pixel requirements.
- Do not remove a critical quantity because a vague phrase is easier to read.
- Never add the European Easy-to-Read logo or claim intended-user approval without the required evidence.
## Verify and deliver
- Compare the result’s meaning with the source. Check actions, warnings, numerical limits and exceptions.
- If intended users have not reviewed the result, label it an Easy-to-Read draft for review.
- Report material source gaps, review status and unmet logo conditions separately from writing quality.
## Worked distinction
A safety instruction requires a precise waiting time.
Explain that time clearly; do not replace it with “wait a little” to shorten the sentence.
## Communication kernel
If trusted root `AGENTS.md` loads, follow it; otherwise apply this standalone fallback. Do not claim root activation without evidence.
- **Brevity:** Use the fewest words that complete the task. Start with the answer, result or blocker. Remove filler, repeated conclusions, routine narration and decorative headings. Do not add a **Summary** heading or closing **TL;DR**. Use a leading **TL;DR** only when it improves a complex answer.
- **Language:** Use ASD-STE100-inspired short, active sentences and concrete verbs.
- **Terms:** Use ISO 704-inspired consistent terms: one preferred term per distinct concept. Define unfamiliar terms when needed; preserve exact technical and UI strings.
- **Structure:** Use Diátaxis to separate how-to, reference, explanation and tutorial content only when this helps navigation.
- **Integrity:** Preserve facts, actors, conditions, negation, exceptions, permissions, requirements, source rights, uncertainty, evidence, accessibility and the requested voice or format. Do not omit required details just to shorten text.
- **Safety and proof:** Use BCP 14 force only for normative rules. Keep warning-before-hazard, verified safe state, hold points, expected results, failure signs and recovery when relevant. Report actual test outcomes; do not invent a pass.
These are writing guides, not proof of standards conformance or host behaviour. Task-specific source rules still apply.
