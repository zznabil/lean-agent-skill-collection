# Inclusion Europe: Easy-to-Read authoring
## Use
- Use this skill for an explicit Easy-to-Read request or when the intended readers and task justify it. Deliver reviewed material or a draft awaiting intended-user review.
- Easy-to-Read is a specialised accessible representation, not merely short text or general plain language.
## Source and scope
- Use Inclusion Europe’s official Information for all booklet. Use the current rules and logo conditions linked in SOURCES.md.
- This pack does not bundle the original PDF. Permission to redistribute it was not established.
- The method requires intended-user involvement. An agent-only wording pass cannot replace that involvement.
## Procedure
1. Identify the intended readers, what they need to know and what they must be able to do.
2. Involve people with relevant intellectual disabilities in planning, drafting or checking, as the method requires.
3. Choose familiar, concrete words. Explain necessary difficult terms. Use the same term for the same concept.
4. State one idea at a time. Use short, clear sentences in a logical order. Identify the actor and action.
5. Use direct, literal statements. Prefer simpler constructions. Do not change prohibitions, exceptions or uncertainty.
6. Use relevant pictures or symbols. They must support the actual meaning and be understandable to the intended readers.
7. Use clear type, consistent alignment and spacing to make the layout easy to follow. Apply the booklet’s rules for the medium.
8. Explain necessary numbers, dates and abbreviations. Retain precise values when they determine safe or correct action.
9. Ask intended readers to explain and use the material. Revise misunderstandings and repeat the affected task check.
10. Check current attribution, intended-user proofreading and logo conditions before claiming Easy-to-Read status or adding the logo.
## Keep the task contract
- Do not infantilise adult readers or assume that they have identical needs. Do not replace the full version without preserving necessary access.
- Do not convert print font sizes directly into arbitrary screen-pixel requirements.
- Do not remove a critical quantity because a vague phrase is easier to read.
- Never add the European Easy-to-Read logo or claim intended-user approval without the required evidence.
## Verify and deliver
- Compare the result’s meaning with the source. Check actions, warnings, numerical limits and exceptions.
- If intended users have not reviewed the result, label it an Easy-to-Read draft for review.
- Report material source gaps, review status and unmet logo conditions separately from writing quality.
## Worked distinction
A safety instruction requires a precise waiting time.
Explain that time clearly; do not replace it with “wait a little” to shorten the sentence.
## Communication kernel
Trusted root `AGENTS.md` governs when loaded; otherwise use this standalone fallback. Do not claim activation without evidence.
Apply this skill’s source-specific procedure only to its task.
- Use ASD-STE100-inspired short, active sentences and concrete words; retain domain terms.
- Use ISO 704-inspired stable concepts and terminology. Define unfamiliar terms when needed.
- Separate tutorial, how-to, reference and explanation with Diátaxis when helpful, not forced headings.
- Lead with the answer or action, not a required Summary. Use TL;DR only first when helpful for complex material, without a repeated conclusion.
- Keep simple answers short, not arbitrarily truncated. Match requested voice and format. Use British spelling except for sources, syntax or requested locale.
- Preserve facts, quantities, negation, actors, conditions, exceptions, rights, permissions, uncertainty and evidence. Never claim unchecked compliance or completion.
- Preserve normative BCP 14 MUST/SHOULD/MAY force and exceptions. Important requirements name one actor, action and observable check.
- Before hazards, warn with verified risks. At critical steps, hold and verify the safe state. Retain task-specific controls.
- When failure is plausible, give expected results, failure signs and recovery.
- For measurable multi-step work, show a named 20-cell ASCII bar (# processed, - remaining), with floor percentage from durable counts.
- Keep PASS/FAIL/BLOCKED separate. Classify failed, blocked, skipped or untested items with evidence before counting.
- Without a defensible total, report phase, evidence and next action. This does not invoke manual wait-what.
- Explain difficult mechanisms from foundations. Use compliant/noncompliant code or configuration contrasts only when useful.
