# Feynman-style explanation: expose the mechanism

## Use
- Use this skill when readers need to understand a difficult concept or when an explanation has not helped them understand. Deliver a correct mechanism and a bounded example.
- Give short answers to simple lookups. Do not assume the reader is a child.

## Source and scope
- Lean’s register and teach/wait-what skills record this informal explanation pattern.
- No canonical formal “Feynman standard” or official skill-specification PDF exists to bundle.
- Check subject-matter claims against relevant task sources. This technique does not supply domain facts.

## Procedure
1. Identify what the reader needs to understand. Identify the prior knowledge that the conversation supports.
2. Explain the mechanism in ordinary language. State what changes, what causes the change and which conditions matter.
3. Define an unavoidable technical term near its use. Do not replace a precise concept with a misleading synonym.
4. Give one concrete example. State its starting facts explicitly and give a correct result.
5. Explain why the important step works. Identify missing links instead of hiding them behind a confident analogy.
6. State the limits of an analogy or example. Keep exceptions to the general rule visible.
7. When learner participation is wanted, inspect a short explanation or transfer attempt. Repair the gap that you observe.
8. Check the mechanism, example result and limits against relevant sources or calculation. Label unresolved claims. Do not hide them in an analogy.

## Keep the task contract
- Retain mathematical conditions, safety distinctions, uncertainty and necessary technical depth.
- Do not treat simple language as proof of truth, completeness or understanding.
- Do not attribute an invented rigid checklist or a numerical learning guarantee to Richard Feynman.
- Do not require a quiz, persona or recap when the user needs only a direct explanation.

## Verify and deliver
- Verify the mechanism, worked example and exception against the actual source or calculation.
- State unresolved knowledge gaps explicitly. Return the explanation. Do not make the technique itself the deliverable.
- Claim observed understanding only when the reader’s performance provides evidence.

## Worked distinction
“A cache remembers answers” is a starting analogy, not a complete description.
State what makes a key match and when an entry becomes invalid. Do not hide these conditions behind the analogy.

## Communication kernel
If trusted root `AGENTS.md` is loaded, its policy governs. Otherwise apply this standalone kernel. If it loads, it governs. Do not claim root activation without evidence. Apply this skill’s source-specific procedure only to its task, not to every reply.
- Use ASD-STE100-inspired short, active technical sentences. Use ISO 704-inspired stable concepts and terminology. Use Diátaxis to separate purposes when helpful. These are communication practices, not claims of formal standards conformance.
- Start with the supported result and next action. Preserve facts, exact negation, actors, conditions, exceptions, rights, permissions, uncertainty, evidence and the requested format. Do not call an unchecked result compliant or complete.
- In normative text, preserve BCP 14 MUST/SHOULD/MAY force and exceptions. For important requirements, identify one actor, the action and an observable check.
- Before a hazardous action, state the verified risk and give a warning. For critical steps, provide a hold point and a safe-state check. When failure is plausible, give the expected result, failure sign and recovery. These controls do not replace task-specific controls.
- For measurable multi-step work, use a named 20-cell ASCII bar (# processed, - remaining). Calculate the floor percentage from durable counts. Keep the PASS/FAIL/BLOCKED verdict separate. Count a failed, blocked, skipped or untested item only after you classify it with evidence. If no defensible total exists, report the phase, evidence and next action without a bar. This does not invoke manual wait-what.
- When you explain a difficult mechanism, start from foundations. For code or configuration, use a compliant/noncompliant contrast only when useful. Do not force examples or sections onto simple tasks.
