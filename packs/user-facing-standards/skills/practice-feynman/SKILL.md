# Feynman-style explanation: expose the mechanism

## Use
- Explain a difficult concept or repair an unsuccessful explanation. Deliver a correct mechanism and bounded example.
- Give short answers to simple lookups. Do not assume the reader is a child.

## Source and scope
- Lean’s register and teach/wait-what skills record this informal explanation pattern.
- No canonical formal “Feynman standard” or official skill-specification PDF exists to bundle.
- Check subject-matter claims against relevant task sources. This technique does not supply domain facts.

## Procedure
1. Identify what the reader needs to understand and the prior knowledge supported by the conversation.
2. Explain the mechanism in ordinary language. State what changes, what causes the change and which conditions matter.
3. Define an unavoidable technical term near its use. Do not replace a precise concept with a misleading synonym.
4. Give one concrete example. State its starting facts explicitly and give a correct result.
5. Explain why the important step works. Identify missing links instead of hiding them behind a confident analogy.
6. State the limits of an analogy or example. Keep exceptions to the general rule visible.
7. When learner participation is wanted, inspect a short explanation or transfer attempt. Repair the gap that you observe.
8. Check the mechanism, example result and limits against relevant sources or calculation. Label unresolved claims, not hide them in analogies.

## Keep the task contract
- Retain mathematical conditions, safety distinctions, uncertainty and necessary technical depth.
- Do not treat simple language as proof of truth, completeness or understanding.
- Do not attribute an invented rigid checklist or a numerical learning guarantee to Richard Feynman.
- Do not require a quiz, persona or recap when the user needs only a direct explanation.

## Verify and deliver
- Verify the mechanism, worked example and exception against the actual source or calculation.
- State unresolved knowledge gaps explicitly. Return the explanation. Do not make the technique itself the deliverable.
- Claim observed understanding only when the reader’s performance provides evidence.

## Worked distinction
“A cache remembers answers” is a starting analogy, not a complete description.
State what makes a key match and when an entry becomes invalid. Do not hide these conditions behind the analogy.
