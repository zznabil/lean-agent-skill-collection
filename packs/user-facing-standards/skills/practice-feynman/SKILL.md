# Feynman-style explanation: expose the mechanism

## Use
- Use when readers need to understand a difficult concept or an explanation has not landed. Deliver a correct mechanism with a bounded example.
- Keep simple lookups short; do not assume the reader is a child.

## Source and scope
- This is the informal explanation pattern recorded in Lean’s register and teach/wait-what skills.
- There is no canonical formal “Feynman standard” or official skill-specification PDF to bundle.
- Verify subject-matter claims against the relevant task sources; the technique is not a source of domain facts.

## Procedure
1. Identify what the reader needs to understand and what prior knowledge is supported by the conversation.
2. State the mechanism in ordinary language: what changes, what causes it and what conditions matter.
3. Define an unavoidable technical term close to its use; do not replace a precise concept with a misleading
  synonym.
4. Give one concrete example with explicit starting facts and a correct result.
5. Explain why the important step works; expose a missing link rather than covering it with a confident analogy.
6. State where an analogy or example stops applying and keep the general rule’s exceptions visible.
7. When learner participation is wanted, inspect a short explanation or transfer attempt and repair the observed
  gap.
8. Check the mechanism, example result and limits against relevant sources or calculation. Label an unresolved claim rather than hiding it in an analogy.

## Keep the task contract
- Preserve mathematical conditions, safety distinctions, uncertainty and necessary technical depth.
- Simple language is not proof of truth, completeness or understanding.
- Do not attribute a rigid invented checklist or numerical learning guarantee to Richard Feynman.
- Do not require a quiz, a persona or a recap when the user only needs a direct explanation.

## Verify and deliver
- Check the mechanism, worked example and exception against the actual source or calculation.
- Identify unresolved knowledge gaps explicitly. Return the explanation, not a performance of the technique.
- Claim observed understanding only when the reader’s performance supplies evidence.

## Worked distinction
“A cache remembers answers” is a starting analogy, not a complete description.
Explain what makes a key match and when an entry becomes invalid; do not hide those conditions behind the analogy.

## Lean communication kernel (standalone fallback)
If root `AGENTS.md` is loaded, it governs. Otherwise apply these rules to communication. This skill’s source-specific procedure runs only for its task, not every reply.
- Lead with the supported result and next action. Use short, active ASD-STE100-inspired technical wording and CDC-style familiar words. Keep how-to, reference and explanation apart when Diátaxis separation helps.
- Preserve facts, exact negation, actors, conditions, exceptions, rights, permissions, uncertainty, evidence and requested format. Never call an unchecked result compliant or complete.
- In normative text, keep BCP 14 MUST/SHOULD/MAY force and exceptions. For important requirements, name one actor, action and observable check (NASA).
- Before a hazardous action, show the verified risk and an ANSI-style warning. For critical steps, use a WHO-style hold point and OSHA-style safe-state check; give the FDA-style expected result, failure sign and recovery when failure is plausible. These analogies do not replace task-specific controls.
- For measurable multi-step work, use a named 20-cell ASCII bar (# processed, - remaining) and floor percentage from durable counts; keep the PASS/FAIL/BLOCKED verdict separate. A failed, blocked, skipped or untested item counts only when classified with evidence. With no defensible total, report phase, evidence and next action without a bar. This does not invoke manual wait-what.
- Explain a difficult mechanism from foundations (Feynman). Use SEI CERT-style compliant/noncompliant contrast for code or configuration only when useful. Do not force examples or sections on simple tasks.
