# IES: organise instruction and study

## Use
- Use for teaching and study plans where learning, retention or transfer is the actual goal.
- Identify the material, prior knowledge, retention horizon and available practice time before selecting techniques.

## Source and scope
- Read references/ies-organizing-instruction-2007.pdf; the complete official 63-page guide is bundled.
- The 2007 guide reports different evidence levels. Do not describe every recommendation as equally established.
- It informs educational judgement; it is not a mandatory script for every explanation or a model-performance
  benchmark.

## Procedure
1. When durable retention matters, space exposure and retrieval over time rather than relying only on one
  concentrated session.
2. Interleave worked solutions with problems learners solve; select support according to current competence.
3. Combine an explanatory graphic with relevant verbal explanation when their relationship helps the learner.
4. Connect concrete examples to the corresponding abstract representation; explain the relationship explicitly.
5. Use retrieval quizzes to re-expose learners to important content and supply corrective feedback.
6. Treat pre-questions before new material as a separate, weaker-evidence recommendation, not the same as retrieval
  practice.
7. Use delayed judgements and tests to help learners identify what needs further study, without treating confidence
  as mastery.
8. Ask a focused explanatory question about why a step or relationship holds, rather than only asking for
  repetition.
9. Review actual answers and transfer attempts; adapt the next example or practice item to the observed
  misunderstanding.

## Preserve the boundary
- The guide rates spacing, worked-example interleaving, graphics/verbal pairing and concrete/abstract pairing as
  Moderate.
- It rates quiz re-exposure and deep explanatory questions as Strong; pre-questions and the two study-allocation
  recommendations as Low.
- These are the guide’s 2007 classifications, not a fresh systematic review or universal effect-size claim.
- Do not invent one optimal spacing interval, impose a quiz on a quick answer or promise retention without
  observation.

## Check and finish
- Match each technique to the learning goal and record which result was actually observed.
- Distinguish immediate performance, delayed retention and independent transfer.
- Return the plan or lesson segment and an appropriate next practice step; label unexecuted assessment as planned.

## Worked distinction
A learner can follow a worked solution but cannot solve a related problem unaided.
Provide a targeted guided attempt and then another transfer check; copying the example does not prove mastery.
