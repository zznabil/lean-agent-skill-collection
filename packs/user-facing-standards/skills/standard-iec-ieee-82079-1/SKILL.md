# IEC/IEEE 82079-1: information for use

## Use
- Use only for the named user-information or interface task below; choose draft, edit or review from the request.
- Use for product instructions, manuals, warnings and support information across the product’s relevant lifecycle.
- Read the target artifact, intended audience, task and authorised scope before changing it.

## Source and scope
- Target edition: IEC/IEEE 82079-1:2019, Edition 2. Official access and source status are in SOURCES.md.
- The full licensed normative text was not available when this prototype was authored and is not bundled.
- The steps below are a Lean application routine based on the published scope and existing Lean guidance, not all
  clauses.
- For clause-level or conformance work, obtain an authorised copy and read the applicable clauses and exceptions
  first.
- Without that copy, do only the supported application work and label the unverified standard-specific requirements.

## Procedure
1. Identify the product version, intended users, intended use, limits, environment and supported lifecycle
  activities.
2. Establish the information users need to install, operate, maintain, troubleshoot and retire the product where
  applicable.
3. Base safety information on verified product hazards and authorised risk controls; do not invent technical safety
  advice.
4. Put prerequisites, required competence and material warnings before the action they constrain.
5. State the action, target object, expected result and available recovery in the sequence users must follow.
6. Choose delivery media, navigation and illustrations that users can access at the point of use.
7. Keep terminology, product identification, revisions and cross-references consistent with the actual product.
8. Have technical and information-design reviewers inspect consequential content against the current product.
9. Evaluate real use with intended readers when practical; correct wording-attributable errors and missing
  information.
10. Assign maintenance ownership so product changes trigger review of the corresponding information for use.

## Preserve the boundary
- Preserve facts, quantities, terms, permissions, prohibitions, conditions, exceptions, warnings and required links.
- Do not invent a requirement, numerical threshold, clause number, user need or evidence to make a checklist look
  complete.
- A review or language edit does not authorise execution, deployment, source acquisition fees or user-data
  collection.
- These human-oriented principles can inform agent instructions, but do not prove how an agent will interpret them.

## Check and finish
- Check the result against the real task, including a relevant failure or recovery path; use intended-user evidence
  when available.
- Separate source-verified clause findings from Lean design suggestions and unchecked items.
- Return the artifact or scoped findings first, followed by material limits and the exact evidence still needed.
- Do not claim formal conformance from this short routine, a readability score or an unexecuted review plan.

## Worked distinction
A maintenance guide lists a hazard only after the hazardous step.
Move the verified warning before that step without changing its technical content or required protective action.
